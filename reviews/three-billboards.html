Screenplay Evaluation · File CW-051

Three Billboards Outside Ebbing, Missouri

DateJune 14, 2026
ProjectThree Billboards Outside Ebbing, Missouri
WriterMartin McDonagh
GenreDark Comedy / Crime Drama
BudgetLow-Medium (Regional Small-Town Locations / Interior Heavy)
StationPASS
Logline

A grieving, unyielding mother rents three abandoned billboards on the edge of her small town to launch a direct, public media indictment against the local police department for failing to identify suspects in her daughter's brutal murder, triggering an escalating war of civic attrition.

Expert Development Analysis

1.The Page 10 Exclusion Zone

The script establishes its high-concept premise early via the rental of the three billboards, but immediately drops its cinematic velocity into an administrative holding pattern. By page 12, the text is entirely consumed by local small-town gossip and office bureaucracy. The protagonist, Mildred, spends her early script real estate delivering extensive, text-heavy sarcastic monologues to local priests and dental workers instead of advancing a forensic inquiry:

MILDREDIt seems to me the local police department is too busy torturing black folks to bother solving a real crime. So those billboards are staying up until you do your job, Chief. CHIEF WILLOUGHBYMildred, I'm doing everything I can. And besides, I've got terminal cancer.

For a commercial crime property, spending the entire first act on conversational posturing and small-town ideological bickering before introducing any active suspect tracking or forensic data logs creates a severe structural bottleneck that threatens immediate reader retention.

2.Narrative Stagnation via the Conversation Loop

The entire second act suffers from a critical pacing paralysis due to its highly formulaic and unstructured layout. The narrative operates almost exclusively as an episodic confrontation loop: Mildred bickers with her abusive ex-husband, an officer throws a local advertising clerk out of a second-story window in broad daylight without facing legal arrest, the police chief leaves long letter assets to his family before committing suicide, a billboard is set on fire, and the cycle resets.

This mechanical staging bogs down the midsection of the script. The text completely rejects basic principles of dialogue economy; characters speak with zero subtext, utilizing lengthy rhetorical flourishes to explain their specific moral worldviews, which completely halts the visual momentum of the script.

3.Complete Lack of Resolution and the Road-Trip Cop-Out

From a development perspective, the script's final act features an incredibly frustrating, commercially toxic structural layout. After enduring an extended cycle of intensive family trauma and civic warfare, the narrative engine completely deflates: the script reveals zero suspects, solves zero crimes, and provides zero structural breakthroughs regarding the daughter's murder case.

Instead, the final resolution relies on the grieving mother and a disgraced, severely burned former officer driving a car toward another state to potentially execute an unverified rapist they met in a diner, explicitly stating they will “decide on the way.” Ending a major theatrical investment on a note of complete open-ended legal and narrative resignation entirely rejects the requirements of commercial resolution.

Mandatory Redlines

  1. Introduce an Active Forensic Track. Rewrite the central premise. Get Mildred or a competent outside investigator involved in tracking active physical evidence or cell data logs by page 15 to anchor the crime elements early.
  2. Enforce Strict Legal Logic. Ensure that extreme criminal acts — such as firebombing an active police precinct or throwing a civilian out of a window — trigger immediate state trooper or federal administrative intervention, protecting baseline credibility.
  3. Re-Engineer the Climax. Rewrite the ending. Eliminate the open-ended road trip cop-out. Force a definitive, high-stakes confrontation or a forensic reveal that provides a clear, marketable resolution of the thriller element rather than an ambiguous conversational fade-out.
